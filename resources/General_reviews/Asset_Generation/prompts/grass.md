Create one clearly recognizable small tuft of **short** meadow grass as a standalone children's storybook illustration.

- **Short blades:** a compact low tuft of many slender blades, each blade reaching only about the lower third to half of the canvas height — like a clipped lawn patch or a small handful of grass, not a tall meadow. The whole tuft stays low and wide.
- **Grassy character:** the blades rise from one compact base and fan out at slightly different angles and heights, some leaning, some nearly upright, with gentle curved sweeps. Several thin stems only, no stems carrying heads.
- Two or three muted greens, with gentle watercolor grain and soft painted variation inside each blade; edges stay soft, no heavy outline.
- Nothing else grows in the tuft: no flowers, buds, seed heads, wheat ears, star-shaped tips, dots, berries or other plant leaves.

Keep the silhouette low, soft and readable at small size, in the Little Play family style.

Center the tuft with comfortable padding and no cropping. It is the only subject. It must read as a **short** tuft of green grass, not wheat, a bush, a flower, a reed or tall grass.

No tall blades, no flowers, no flower heads, no petals, no star-shaped tips, no buds, no seed heads, no berries, no insects, no field, soil, landscape, text, logo, face, glossy 3D rendering, photorealism, emoji/vector styling, heavy outline, glow or extra tufts.
