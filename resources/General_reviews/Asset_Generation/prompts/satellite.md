--- prompt ---

Create one clearly recognizable simple artificial satellite as a standalone children's storybook illustration. The silhouette must read as a satellite at a glance, even at card size:

- A small, flat, boxy or short-cylindrical central body — clearly NOT a ball or sphere.
- Two LARGE rectangular solar-panel wings, one on each side, attached by short thin booms, each wing split into a visible grid of 3x2 or 4x2 panel cells. The panel wings are the widest part of the picture and make the silhouette instantly a satellite.
- One small round dish antenna mounted on top of the body, tilted slightly up.
- One short thin antenna rod detail and a small thruster nozzle at the back.

Colours: muted blue-grey body, deep blue solar panels with pale grid lines, warm cream and gentle golden accents. Soft watercolor grain, rounded construction, gentle painted shading and no heavy outline.

Center the complete satellite with comfortable padding. It must read as a satellite, not a spaceship, rocket, airplane, drone, or a ball with straps around it.

No spherical or ball-shaped body, no fabric, straps or belts wrapped around the body, no balloons, no planets, stars, sky, astronaut, rocket, text, logo, face, scene, glossy 3D rendering, photorealism, emoji/vector styling, heavy black outline, glow or extra objects.
