--- prompt ---

Create one standalone illustration of a single simple pickaxe for a cozy early-childhood storybook game. The first two reference images are PHOTOS of real pickaxes: copy their SHAPE and PROPORTIONS (the crescent head, central collar with oval hole, pointed pick, flat adze blade, handle) but render it in the painted storybook style of the other references — NOT photorealistic. Match the supplied Little Play bear and bone references for the painted finish: soft watercolor wash with very subtle paper/colored-pencil grain; warm natural, gently muted colors; rounded, slightly organic shapes; soft painted shading; restrained contrast; quiet handmade charm; minimal, clean details; readable silhouette at small mobile size.

Draw one simple pickaxe: a short wooden handle with a heavy forged-steel head mounted on top. The steel head forms a gentle crescent (a sweeping arch, not a straight line), with a thick central collar (the "eye") that has a vertical oval hole through it where the handle passes. From one side of the collar extends a LONG arm that tapers down significantly and curves slightly downward to a very sharp, elongated, four-sided pyramid point (the pick). The point must be LONG and POINTY — a slender, tapered, sharp spike, not a short stubby tip. From the opposite side extends a matching arm that flattens out horizontally into a sharp, flat, wedge-like blade (the adze/chisel), also with a longer reach. The overall silhouette is a crescent-shaped metal head on a wooden handle with a LONG pointed pick on one side and a longer flat blade on the other — clearly a pickaxe, NOT a star, NOT a T-shape, NOT a hammer. The pick tip must be noticeably longer and pointier than before.

The steel head is dark grey/charcoal/matte black with subtle rough dimples and ripples (hammered texture). The very tips of both the point and the flat blade are ground down, showing shiny polished silver raw metal where sharpened. The central collar is thick and bulky compared to the arms. The oval hole is slightly wider at the top than the bottom.

Paint the handle a soft, warm brown (wood). Paint the steel head a dark grey/charcoal/matte black with subtle hammered dimples and ripples. The very tips of both the point and the flat blade are shiny polished silver (ground/sharpened). Paint one soft lighter highlight and a slightly deeper shading so the head reads as rounded and dimensional without looking glossy or 3D. Keep the colors clearly readable when the picture is shrunk to a small card.

Keep it a simple, calm object picture with no face, no eyes and no expression. No hand, no arm, no extra tool. The pickaxe must read instantly as a pickaxe at small size and must not look like a hammer, an axe or a tool blob.

One subject only, fully visible, centered with comfortable breathing room. Plain warm off-white background is acceptable. No added props, no ground line, no other object. The result must look like it belongs beside the exact reference images.

No photorealism, 3D rendering, glossy plastic, vector emoji, stock-icon look, or anime styling. Not a hammer, not an axe, not a generic tool, not a star, not a star shape, not a T-shape. No thick black outline, white sticker border, glow, hard drop shadow, or dramatic lighting. No excessive gradient, tiny detail, heavy texture, decorative sparkles, or ornamental extras. No unrequested face or accessories. No text, labels, watermark, logo, collage, contact sheet, or multiple alternatives in one image. Do not reproduce the reference bear or the bone; create the requested pickaxe in the same visual family.
