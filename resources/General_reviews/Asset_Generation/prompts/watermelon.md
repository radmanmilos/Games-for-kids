Create one standalone illustration of a single simple watermelon slice for a cozy early-childhood storybook game. Match the supplied Little Play bear, golden star and bone references: soft watercolor wash with very subtle paper/colored-pencil grain; warm natural, gently muted colors; rounded, slightly organic shapes; soft painted shading; restrained contrast; quiet handmade charm; minimal, clean details; readable silhouette at small mobile size.

Draw one triangular watermelon slice: a wedge shape with a curved outer edge (the rind) and a pointed inner edge (the center), like a slice cut from a round melon. Keep the silhouette compact and instantly readable as a watermelon slice.

Fill the flesh with a clear, unmistakable watermelon red — a soft, slightly deep pinkish-red, clearly red and never brown, grey, or muddy. Keep the colors clearly readable when the picture is shrunk to a small card.

Keep it a simple, calm object picture with no face, no eyes and no expression. The slice must read instantly as a watermelon at small size and must not look like a pizza slice, a triangle, a strawberry or a red blob.

One subject only, fully visible, centered with comfortable breathing room. Plain warm off-white background is acceptable. No added props, no plate, no knife, no whole melon, no other fruit, no ground line. The result must look like it belongs beside the exact reference images.

No photorealism, 3D rendering, glossy plastic, vector emoji, stock-icon look, or anime styling. Not a brown slice, not a grey slice, not a pizza slice. No thick black outline, white sticker border, glow, hard drop shadow, or dramatic lighting. No excessive gradient, tiny detail, heavy texture, decorative sparkles, or ornamental extras. No unrequested face or accessories. No text, labels, watermark, logo, collage, contact sheet, or multiple alternatives in one image.
