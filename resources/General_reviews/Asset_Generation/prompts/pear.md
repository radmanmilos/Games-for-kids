Create one standalone illustration of a single simple pear for a cozy early-childhood storybook game. Match the supplied Little Play bear, golden star and bone references: soft watercolor wash with very subtle paper/colored-pencil grain; warm natural, gently muted colors; rounded, slightly organic shapes; soft painted shading; restrained contrast; quiet handmade charm; minimal, clean details; readable silhouette at small mobile size.

Draw one pear with its classic silhouette: a rounded, full, bulbous bottom that narrows into a shorter, slimmer neck and a small stem at the top. The shape must clearly read as a pear and not as an apple — the bottom is heavier and wider than the top. Keep the silhouette compact and instantly readable.

Fill it with a clear, unmistakable pear green — a soft, warm, slightly yellowish green, clearly green and never brown, grey, blue, or muddy. The green must be obvious and definite. Paint one small short stem at the top, simple and brownish-green. Do not add leaves unless tiny; keep the focus on the fruit. Do not paint individual skin pores or texture; keep the surface softly painted and clean.

Keep it a simple, calm object picture with no face, no eyes and no expression. The pear must read instantly as a pear at small size and must not look like an apple, a lightbulb, a gourd or a green blob.

One subject only, fully visible, centered with comfortable breathing room. Plain warm off-white background is acceptable. No added props, no plate, no knife, no other fruit, no ground line. The result must look like it belongs beside the exact reference images.

No thick black outline, white sticker border, glow, hard drop shadow, or dramatic lighting. No excessive gradient, tiny detail, heavy texture, decorative sparkles, or ornamental extras. No unrequested face or accessories. No text, labels, watermark, logo, collage, contact sheet, or multiple alternatives in one image.
